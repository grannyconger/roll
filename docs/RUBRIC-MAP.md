# Rubric map

All 19 indicators of SCTS 4.0 outside the Professionalism domain, mapped to the
evidence that carries them. Target is Proficient, a 3, on every one.

Three sources of evidence:

- **Built** means the app produces it whether or not anything goes right.
- **Taught** means a facilitation move in `FACILITATION.md`.
- **Written** means the planning documents, which are scored on paper.

---

## Instruction, 12 indicators

### Standards and Objectives

Proficient wants most objectives communicated, sub-objectives aligned, objectives
connected to prior learning, clear expectations, standards displayed and referenced,
and evidence most students demonstrate mastery.

- **Built.** The standard and objective are posted on the display and on every
  student screen for the full 90 minutes. The score distribution panel is live
  mastery evidence, visible to the observer in real time.
- **Taught.** The objective is referenced at every transition and returned to in
  closure. Prior learning is named: the investment vehicles unit supplied the asset
  types, this lesson supplies the behavior.
- **Written.** Seven sub-objectives sequenced basic to complex in `LESSON-PLAN.md`.
  The expectation of Level 3 on 3 of 4 is stated to students and posted.

### Motivating Students

Content organized to be personally meaningful and relevant, inquiry and curiosity
valued, effort reinforced.

- **Built.** A game with real suspense, personal stakes, and a reveal the whole room
  watches together. The Learn to Play practice rounds let students discover the loop
  rather than hear it.
- **Taught.** Effort is reinforced through the standing rule that reasoning is scored
  and balance is not, which is stated at the start and demonstrated when a strong OUT
  response is spotlighted next to a strong IN.

### Presenting Instructional Content

Visuals that establish purpose and include internal summaries, examples and analogies
for new concepts, modeling, concise communication, logical sequencing, no irrelevant
information.

- **Built.** The display follows classroom slide rules: one idea per screen,
  lecture-hall readable, no decoration. The agenda popup is an internal summary a
  student can open at any time.
- **Taught.** The lawyer analogy for CER. The think-aloud modeling beat in the
  mini-lesson, including a deliberately weak version the class critiques. The
  batting-average analogy for expected value.

### Lesson Structure and Pacing

Starts promptly, coherent beginning middle end and reflection, pacing appropriate,
opportunities for students at different rates, efficient material routines, little
time lost in transitions.

- **Built.** Phase transitions are instant across all screens with nothing to pass
  out or collect. Timers are explicit. Fast finishers receive an extension prompt on
  their own screen instead of idling. Five minutes of reserve are planned.
- **Taught.** Door greeting and an immediate sign-in instruction mean the lesson
  starts at minute zero. Chime resets rather than talking over the room.
- **Written.** The Thinking Log is pre-placed before the bell, so the only paper in
  the lesson costs zero transition time.

### Activities and Materials

Support objectives, challenging, sustain attention, elicit varied thinking, time for
reflection, relevant to students' lives, student-to-student interaction, induce
curiosity and suspense, provide choices, incorporate technology, resources beyond the
textbook.

This is the strongest indicator in the lesson and reaches Exemplary territory on
several lines.

- **Built.** Game-like, simulation-based, technology-enabled, with choice in every
  round and in which extra round to write about. The reveal sequence is suspense by
  construction.
- **Taught.** Team deliberation is sustained student-to-student interaction with real
  disagreement. Peer scoring is a second interaction structure.
- **Written.** NGPF materials, a teacher-built application, and an external
  vocabulary tool, none of them a course textbook.

### Questioning

Varied question types across knowledge, application, and creation, purposeful and
coherent, moderate frequency, sequenced to goals, active responses such as whole-class
signaling or written responses, wait time often provided, volunteers and non-
volunteers balanced.

- **Built.** **Every student responds to every prompt on their own device.** This is
  the written and whole-class active response the indicator asks for, with no
  hand-raising bottleneck. The hub randomizer draws from students not yet called on,
  so balance is structural rather than remembered.
- **Taught.** Three-second wait time after every question, specified at each question
  in the script. Volunteers first, then pick. Question types span all three
  categories, with expected answers written out.

### Academic Feedback

Mostly academically focused, frequent, high quality, given during guided practice,
teacher circulates to monitor and support, feedback from students used to adjust
instruction, students give feedback to one another.

The strongest single case in the lesson.

- **Built.** Specific criterion-referenced written feedback on all 9 responses per
  student, delivered in seconds. The hub flags Level 1 students so circulation is
  targeted rather than random. The distribution panel lets the mini-lesson be
  adjusted live based on Game 1 results.
- **Taught.** Constant circulation, with the two minutes after Game 1 reserved for
  flagged students specifically.
- **Built, again.** Peer scoring covers students giving feedback to one another
  against the same criteria.

### Grouping Students

Arrangements enhance understanding and learning efficiency, most students know roles
and expectations, most held accountable for group and individual work, composition
varied.

- **Built.** Teams are constructed from Game 1 decision data so each holds four
  different strategies, with reasoning strength spread evenly. Composition is varied
  by construction and the rationale is data-driven rather than arbitrary. Arrangements
  move through whole class, teams, and individual across the period.
- **Taught.** The archetype is a named seat with a known responsibility: defend the
  approach you actually played. Accountability is both group, the four rounds decided
  together, and individual, every student's own responses scored separately.

### Teacher Content Knowledge

Accurate content, subject-specific strategies implemented regularly, key concepts
highlighted and used to connect other ideas.

- **Taught.** Expected value derived live from the actual dice distribution.
  Independence of events addressed when a student raises the streak argument.
  Behavioral finance concepts named precisely and connected to what students just
  felt.
- **Built.** Seven mind traps defined in student-facing vocabulary and surfaced at
  the moment students have experienced them.

### Teacher Knowledge of Students

Displays understanding of most students' anticipated difficulties, incorporates
interests and cultural background, regularly provides differentiated methods and
content.

- **Written.** Anticipated difficulties are named in advance in `LESSON-PLAN.md`:
  subtraction errors when IN during a loss, the recency fallacy, and writing that
  stops at a feeling.
- **Built.** Mental math scaffolds on a wrong answer before revealing. Feedback is
  differentiated per student by definition. Dyslexia font, one-task-at-a-time, and
  explicit timers are defaults rather than accommodations granted on request.

### Thinking

Proficient requires two types of thinking taught thoroughly, plus regular
opportunities to generate ideas and analyze problems from multiple perspectives.

Three types are present, which exceeds the bar.

- **Analytical.** Comparing weak and strong justifications, evaluating peer work
  against criteria, analyzing the odds.
- **Practical.** Applying expected value to a live decision with money on the line.
- **Research-based.** Reviewing multiple strategies in the team huddle and evaluating
  which hold up.
- **Multiple perspectives** is built into the team design: every student must argue
  their approach to three people who played differently.

### Problem Solving

Proficient requires two problem-solving types taught and reinforced.

Four are present.

- **Drawing conclusions and justifying solutions.** The entire CER spine.
- **Predicting outcomes.** Every IN or OUT decision under uncertainty.
- **Improving solutions.** Revising a justification after feedback, between games.
- **Identifying relevant and irrelevant information.** Distinguishing evidence from
  feelings, and a streak from a probability.

## Planning, 3 indicators

Scored from the documents, not the observed period.

### Instructional Plans

Goals aligned to standards, activities aligned and sequenced basic to complex,
building on prior knowledge, appropriate time for student work and closure, evidence
the plan suits the learners, opportunities to accommodate individual needs.

- **Written.** `LESSON-PLAN.md` carries the standard, seven sequenced sub-objectives,
  the prior-knowledge connection, a minute-level sequence with closure and reserve
  time, and a differentiation section written in advance.

### Student Work

Assignments require students to interpret and analyze rather than reproduce, draw
conclusions and support them through writing, connect learning to prior learning and
life experience.

- **Built.** Nine written justifications per student, none of which can be answered
  by recall. The exit ticket is pure transfer to a situation outside the game.
- **Taught.** The connection to real investing behavior is named in the debrief and
  in closure.

### Assessment

Aligned with standards, clear measurement criteria, performance measured in more than
two ways, written tasks required, performance checks throughout.

- **Written.** Five measurement modes documented in `LESSON-PLAN.md`: written
  justifications, mental math, game decisions, peer review, and the exit ticket. One
  criterion set, the 4-level guide, used across every written mode and visible to
  students the whole period. Performance checks run every round rather than at the
  end.

## Environment, 4 indicators

### Expectations

Clear and rigorous academic expectations for every student, aligned materials and
resources, students encouraged to learn from mistakes, opportunities where all
students can experience success, work completed to expectations.

- **Built.** The scoring guide is visible all period, so the standard for success is
  never hidden. Because reasoning is scored and balance is not, a student who loses
  every round can still reach Level 4, which means success is genuinely available to
  everyone.
- **Taught.** Learning from mistakes is the structure of the lesson. Game 1 is
  supposed to produce weak writing, and students revise against feedback in Game 2.
  Trying something new is explicitly safe.

### Engaging Students and Managing Behavior

Students mostly engaged and on task, clear rules and commitments, several techniques
maintaining engagement and a positive environment, positive behavior recognized,
individuals addressed rather than the class.

- **Built.** Every student responds every round, so disengagement is visible in the
  counter rather than invisible behind a screen. The leaderboard stays off by
  default, which removes the main driver of off-task comparison.
- **Taught.** Constant circulation is the primary control for off-task browsing.
  Individual redirection, never whole-class. Chime rather than raised voice.

### Environment

Welcomes all students and guests, organized to promote learning for all, supplies
accessible, displays current student work, arranged for individual and group learning.

- **Built.** **Current student work is displayed live.** The Spotlight feature puts
  real student responses from this period on the board, which is a literal match to
  the indicator and is rare to see in the moment rather than on a back wall.
- **Taught.** Door greeting welcomes students and any guest. The room moves from
  individual seating to team tables and back, arranged for both.
- **Built.** The observer view is itself a welcome to a guest, giving the evaluator
  access to what is happening rather than leaving him to infer it.

### Respectful Culture

Interactions generally positive and reflect consideration of all students'
backgrounds, respect and kindness both directions, classroom free of unhealthy
conflict, sarcasm, and put-downs, teacher receptive to student interests and opinions.

- **Built.** The leaderboard is off by default, which is the single biggest
  protection against put-downs in a competitive game. Preferred names mean every
  student is addressed as they choose to be. Peer review is anonymous, so critique
  lands on the writing and never on the person. No student's score is ever shown to
  another student.
- **Taught.** No public shaming is a standing rule. Flagged students are visited
  privately, never named. When a student argues the streak position, it is taken
  seriously and returned to later rather than corrected flat.

## Summary

| Domain | Indicators | Carried primarily by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instruction | 12 | Built and taught together, with Questioning and Academic Feedback carried mostly by the app |
| Planning | 3 | Written documents only |
| Environment | 4 | Built defaults plus standing facilitation rules |

The features most at risk if cut, in order: peer scoring, which is the only
student-to-student feedback evidence. Spotlight, which is the only live display of
current student work. The leaderboard default-off setting, which protects Respectful
Culture. The probe, which carries the strongest Thinking evidence in the lesson.
